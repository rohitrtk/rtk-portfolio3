## Overview

Opening Bell is a pre-market research dashboard that turns recent financial
news and market data into a ranked watchlist of stocks and ETFs. It separates
three ideas that are often conflated: how much attention a ticker is receiving,
its likely direction, and the confidence behind that signal.

The application is intended to help organize morning research. Its rankings
surface candidates for further investigation rather than making price
predictions or providing financial advice.

## The problem

Pre-market research requires moving between news feeds, quotes, charts, and
company data while deciding which stories are actually material. A ticker can
appear frequently without having a clear directional catalyst, and one strong
headline can be less convincing than several independent sources reporting the
same development.

I wanted a workflow that could answer three questions quickly:

- Which tickers are attracting meaningful attention this morning?
- Does the available reporting suggest a positive, negative, or mixed catalyst?
- What evidence contributed to the ranking?

## The solution

### A ranked morning brief

The main dashboard summarizes the tone of recent market coverage and ranks the
most relevant tickers. Each row keeps the core signal visible: recent price
movement, attention, likely direction, confidence, and the leading catalyst.

### Explainable signals

Selecting a ticker opens a detailed view of the signal. The contributing news
stories remain attached to the result so a user can inspect the evidence,
compare sources, and decide whether the ranking deserves more research.

### Market and technical context

A signal is more useful when it can be viewed alongside the underlying asset.
Opening Bell combines company information, valuation and volume metrics, daily
ranges, and price history in the same drawer.

## Signal pipeline

The API groups recent articles by ticker, removes unsupported or low-relevance
items, and scores the remaining coverage using several factors:

- article recency and ticker relevance;
- the number and breadth of reporting sources;
- sentiment direction and intensity;
- the concentration of positive, negative, or mixed coverage.

Attention, direction, and confidence are calculated separately. This prevents
a heavily discussed company from automatically being presented as a strong
directional opportunity.

## Technical approach

The React and TypeScript frontend uses a focused dashboard layout, interactive
charts, and a lazily loaded candlestick chart. A FastAPI backend handles news
aggregation, signal calculation, market-data retrieval, and response
validation.

The backend combines news and sentiment data with quotes, profiles, and
historical candles. Price history is requested in bulk where possible to avoid
performing a separate network request for every ticker in the watchlist.

## Reliability and performance

External market-data providers can be slow or temporarily unavailable, so the
application applies different cache windows to news, quotes, candles, and
company profiles. Concurrent requests share in-flight work through asynchronous
locks, which prevents a dashboard refresh from producing duplicate upstream
requests.

When fresh data is unavailable, the API can fall back to recent cached data or
a demo response. The interface communicates the data source so degraded
operation does not look like a live market result.

## Key tradeoffs

- News sentiment is an input to research, not a forecast of future returns.
- Source coverage and provider limits can affect which tickers are surfaced.
- A simple, explainable scoring model is easier to inspect than an opaque model,
  but it still requires judgment from the user.
- Caching improves reliability, while accepting that some views may be several
  minutes behind the latest provider response.

## Outcome

Opening Bell brings discovery, evidence, company context, and price history
into one pre-market workflow. The project also gave me an opportunity to design
an explainable ranking system and build a resilient integration around
rate-limited third-party financial data.

## What I would improve next

- Add saved watchlists and configurable signal thresholds.
- Track how signals change throughout a trading session.
- Expand provider fallbacks and data-quality monitoring.
- Add historical evaluation tools without presenting past results as predictive.
